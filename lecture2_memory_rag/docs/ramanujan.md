# Srinivasa Ramanujan

Srinivasa Ramanujan (22 December 1887 – 26 April 1920) was an Indian mathematician who, with almost no formal training, made extraordinary contributions to number theory, infinite series, and continued fractions.

## The Letter to Hardy

In early 1913, Ramanujan sent a nine-page letter to G.H. Hardy at Trinity College, Cambridge. Hardy received the letter on **January 16, 1913**. It contained 120 statements of theorems, presented without proofs. Hardy later described it as one of the most remarkable letters he had ever received, and immediately arranged for Ramanujan to come to Cambridge.

## The Notebooks

Ramanujan kept **three notebooks** during his lifetime, now held at the University of Madras and Cambridge. These notebooks contain approximately **3,909 entries** — theorems, formulas, and identities, most stated without proof. A fourth notebook, discovered in 1976 at Trinity College Library, is called the "Lost Notebook" and contains 138 pages of results written in the last year of his life.

## Later Life

Ramanujan was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1918, only the second Indian to receive this honor. He returned to India in 1919 in poor health and died in Madras on **26 April 1920**, aged 32. His results on mock theta functions, partition theory, and highly composite numbers continue to influence research today.
