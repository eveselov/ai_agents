# The Basel Problem

The Basel problem asks for the exact sum of the reciprocals of the squares of the positive integers:

    1 + 1/4 + 1/9 + 1/16 + ... = Σ(1/n²)

## History

The problem was first posed by **Pietro Mengoli** in **1650**. For over 80 years it resisted all attempts at solution. Leibniz and the Bernoulli brothers computed numerical approximations — the sum converges to about 1.6449 — but no one could identify the closed form.

## Euler's Solution

In **1734**, Leonhard Euler, then **28 years old** and working at the St. Petersburg Academy of Sciences, announced a solution: the sum equals **π²/6**. His method was audacious and non-rigorous by modern standards: he treated the infinite Taylor series of sin(x)/x as if it were a finite polynomial, identified its roots with multiples of π, and expanded the product form to extract the coefficient of x².

Euler published the full derivation in his paper **"De summis serierum reciprocarum"** (*On the sums of series of reciprocals*) in **1735**. Rigorous justifications were provided decades later, but the result itself was correct.

## Significance

The Basel problem established the unexpected bridge between π and the sum of integer reciprocals. It opened the door to Euler's study of the zeta function ζ(s), which Riemann later generalized into one of the most famous unsolved problems in mathematics.
