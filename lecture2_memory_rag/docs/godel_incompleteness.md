# Gödel's Incompleteness Theorems

In **1931**, Kurt Gödel published a paper that permanently changed the foundations of mathematics. The paper — **"Über formal unentscheidbare Sätze der Principia Mathematica und verwandter Systeme I"** (*On formally undecidable propositions of Principia Mathematica and related systems I*) — appeared in *Monatshefte für Mathematik und Physik* when Gödel was **24 years old**.

## The Two Theorems

**First Incompleteness Theorem**: Any consistent formal system powerful enough to express basic arithmetic contains true statements that cannot be proved within the system.

**Second Incompleteness Theorem**: Such a system cannot prove its own consistency.

## The Method: Gödel Numbering

Gödel's key technique was to encode statements *about* the formal system *into* the system itself. He assigned a unique integer — a **Gödel number** — to every symbol, formula, and proof sequence. This made it possible to write a statement that, in effect, says "this statement cannot be proved." The self-reference parallels the ancient Liar's Paradox ("this sentence is false"), but made mathematically precise.

## Historical Context

Gödel announced the First Theorem at a conference in **Königsberg on September 7, 1930** — the day before David Hilbert gave his famous retirement address declaring: *"We must know, we will know."* The theorems effectively ended **Hilbert's Program**, which had sought to establish all of mathematics on a complete and consistent formal foundation.
